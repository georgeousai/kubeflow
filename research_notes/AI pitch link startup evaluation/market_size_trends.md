Research date: 9 October 2026. Method note: direct page fetches failed in this session (DNS errors on carta.com, crunchbase.com and fred.stlouisfed.org). Most numbers below come from search-engine extracts of the cited pages, not from reading the full pages. ## 1. Top-down: size and growth of the adjacent markets

### Takeaway
None of the analyst categories matches this product exactly, and the estimates disagree with each other by 2-8x. The adjacent markets add up to about $1B (AI video generators) up to $13-17B (conversational AI) in 2025, with 12-33% CAGRs. The money that matters for a solo founder is the small prosumer/SMB slice where companies like Gamma, HeyGen and Synthesia each reached about $100-150M ARR in 2025. That slice is a better measure of real demand than the headline TAMs.

### Inferences
- The categories overlap and cannot be added together. A defensible "adjacent TAM" statement would be: "the product sits at the intersection of presentation software (about $7-13B), sales enablement (about $2.4-6B), conversational AI (about $13-17B) and AI avatars/video (about $0.8-6B), each growing about 12-33% per year (2025 analyst estimates)." The serviceable piece is far smaller.
- The best evidence of real willingness to pay is company ARR, not analyst TAMs. Three horizontal AI content tools (Gamma, Synthesia, HeyGen) each reached about $100-150M ARR by late 2025. Gamma's numbers (600k payers out of 70M users, about 0.86% paid conversion, about $14/month blended ARPU implied by $100M / 600k / 12) are a useful template for a self-serve prosumer tool.
- Analyst CAGRs for avatars and video (20-33%) are well below the ARR growth of the leading vendors from end-2024 to late 2025: about 1.65x for Synthesia and HeyGen, and about 3.3x for Gamma (Sacra estimates). Spend is concentrating in a few winners rather than spreading across many small products.

### Gaps
- No analyst report sizes the interactive demo or "shareable content room" market (Navattic, Storylane, Arcade, DocSend) on its own. Only vendor usage metrics were found.
- No report sizes "AI pitch/investor materials" as a category.
- Grand View Research's conversational AI figure ($41.39B by 2030, 23.7% CAGR) appeared in search summaries, but the originating page could not be confirmed, so it is excluded.
- Analyst methodologies (survey sizes, inclusion rules) were not visible in the search extracts. All analyst numbers should be treated as directional.

## 2. Bottom-up inputs: how many potential buyers exist, and a realistic SAM/SOM

### Takeaway
The "fundraising founder" segment is small and episodic. US startups issue about 50k pre-seed SAFEs/notes a year on Carta, and priced seed rounds number in the low thousands on Carta. Counts are falling even as dollar totals rise. The large pool is SMBs, independents and LinkedIn company pages: 36.2M US small businesses, 27.6M US full-time independents and about 71M LinkedIn company pages. A solo founder's realistic SOM is a few hundred paying accounts, about $10-30k MRR, and churn is the binding constraint.

### Inferences (illustrative model; assumptions flagged)
- **Segment A, fundraising founders (US).** Carta's 50,316 pre-seed instruments (2025) plus about 1,000-1,300 Carta seed rounds suggest the number of US companies actively raising pre-seed/seed in a year is in the low tens of thousands. Assumption: instruments per company average 2-4, and Carta covers a large but incomplete share of US startups. Global might be 2-3x the US (unsourced assumption). These buyers are episodic: a fundraise lasts about 3-6 months, which caps lifetime value. Deal counts are falling (Carta -13% in 2025, -22% YoY in Q2 2026; Crunchbase sub-$5M seed -20%), so this segment is shrinking in count even as dollars grow.
- **Segment B, SMB/B2B marketers, consultants and agencies.** Pool: 36.2M US small businesses, 27.6M US full-time independents and about 71M LinkedIn company pages globally. Illustrative SAM: if 0.5-1% of LinkedIn company pages (about 350k-710k orgs) are active B2B marketers who would pay $20-50/month for an AI "explainer + Q&A" page, SAM is about $85M-$425M ARR. This is an assumption-driven number, not sourced.
- **SOM for a solo founder.** $10k MRR needs about 345 customers at $29/month, about 204 at $49/month or about 101 at $99/month. That is under 0.1% of the illustrative SAM, so market size is not the constraint. Retention and distribution are (see section 6).
- **Churn math.** ChartMogul's AI-native median gross revenue retention is about 40% per year (about 23% for products under $50/month); see section 6. That implies about 7.4%/month (40%) to 11.5%/month (23%) revenue churn. Holding 300 customers at 11.5% monthly churn needs about 35 new paying customers per month. At a Gamma-like 0.9% free-to-paid conversion, that is about 3,900 signups/month. Pricing at $50-100+/month aimed at teams with an ongoing need (sales, agencies, B2B marketing) improves this materially compared with one-off fundraising founders.

### Gaps
- No reliable global count of startups founded per year. GEM gives rates only, and the often-quoted "X million startups per year" figures have no traceable methodology.
- No full-year 2025 Carta seed round count, and no PitchBook/Crunchbase global seed deal count. The full reports could not be fetched.
- No official LinkedIn MAU. The company-page count (71M) is secondary.
- No data on how many LinkedIn company pages belong to active B2B companies rather than dormant pages.
- No count of "agencies" or "creators" relevant to B2B content. Creator-economy numbers were not researched because they mostly cover consumer creators.

## 3. Spending benchmarks: what founders and SMBs pay for adjacent tools

### Takeaway
Self-serve prices for adjacent tools cluster at $10-65 per seat per month (Gamma, Canva, DocSend, Arcade, Storylane Starter). Team or "growth" tiers that unlock analytics, branding and integrations run $150-$1,500 per month (DocSend Advanced, Storylane Growth/Premium, Navattic Base). Conversational video agents are usage-priced (Tavus $59-$397/month for 100-1,250 minutes). A new product can realistically charge $19-49/month self-serve and $99-499/month for team or sales use.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- A 1-5 person startup probably spends about $20-150/month across deck, document-sharing and video tools combined. That is an inference from the per-seat prices above; no survey measures it. The product competes for that same budget line.
- A clean price ladder would be: free (watermarked link, capped Q&A minutes), about $29/month (Gamma Pro, DocSend Standard and Canva Pro are $15-65), about $99-199/month for teams with analytics and CRM export (DocSend Advanced is $150), and usage add-ons for voice or avatar minutes. Tavus and HeyGen show avatar minutes cannot be bundled unlimited.

### Gaps
- No primary survey of what pre-seed/seed founders actually spend per month on fundraising tools (deck, data room, video). Only list prices were found.
- Current Pitch.com, HeyGen self-serve plan and Synthesia self-serve prices were not retrieved.
- Intercom Fin, Drift or Qualified pricing for AI website agents was not retrieved.

## 4. Trends: AI unit costs (video, TTS/voice agents, interactive avatars)

### Takeaway
Voice-agent costs have fallen sharply (OpenAI realtime audio: -60% in Dec 2024, about -20-25% again in Aug 2025) and now run about $0.06-0.42/min all-in. Generative video is still expensive: $1.80-$12 per minute of output on Veo 3.1, and $6+/min on Sora 2. Real-time talking avatars cost about $0.18-1.09/min. A slide-plus-voiceover explainer is cheap to serve, but live avatar Q&A carries real variable cost per visitor and must be gated.

### Cited Findings
- OpenAI Realtime: the Dec 2024 snapshot cut audio token prices 60% to $40/1M input and $80/1M output, and cached audio input fell 87.5% to $2.50/1M — [OpenAI announcement via mirror](https://redlib.hbubli.cc/r/OpenAI/comments/1hgxz8e/realtime_api_costs_since_update). gpt-realtime (Aug 2025) was about 25% cheaper than its predecessor per one integrator — [Chat Data changelog](https://www.chat-data.com/changelog/gpt-realtime-launch)
- 2026 realtime pricing conflicts. One July 2026 guide lists gpt-realtime-2.1 at $32/$64 per 1M audio tokens, about $0.077/min of assistant speech (flagship) and $0.024 (mini), with $0.06-0.11/min all-in when using caching — [Forasoft](https://www.forasoft.com/blog/article/openai-realtime-api-pricing). Another listing gives $10/$20 per 1M and about $0.117/min for the mini — [CallMissed](https://www.callmissed.com/models/gpt-realtime-2-1-mini)
- ElevenLabs Agents hosting is $0.08/min — [CloudZero](https://www.cloudzero.com/blog/elevenlabs-pricing/). All-in voice agent cost is $0.12-0.42/min including LLM, STT, TTS and telephony, per a competitor source — [TRTC](https://trtc.io/blog/details/conversational-ai-pricing-2026)
- Generative video APIs (third-party aggregators, 2026): Veo 3.1 Lite 720p $0.03/sec, Veo 3.1 Fast 720p $0.08/sec, Veo 3.1 standard $0.20/sec. Sora 2 is $0.10/sec (720p), Sora 2 Pro $0.30-0.50/sec, and batch tier is about 50% off — [CometAPI](https://www.cometapi.com/ai-video-api-pricing/); [Apiframe](https://apiframe.ai/blog/ai-video-api-pricing-2026)

- Interactive avatars: Tavus $0.37 per real-time conversation minute (includes LLM, TTS and WebRTC); HeyGen interactive about $0.18-0.78/min; premium real-time avatar stacks $0.56-1.09/min all-in, with avatar rendering the largest cost — [Forasoft](https://www.forasoft.com/blog/article/heygen-alternatives); [LemonSlice avatar API roundup, Sep 2026](https://lemonslice.com/i/best-avatar-api)
- Demand signal for avatars: analyst CAGRs of 29-44% (section 1), and Synthesia/HeyGen ARR growth of about 65% from end-2024 to Sep 2025 (Synthesia $88M to $146M; HeyGen $57.5M to $95M) per Sacra — [Sacra Synthesia](https://sacra.com/c/synthesia); [Sacra HeyGen](https://sacra.com/c/heygen)

### Inferences
- Unit economics per visitor: a 3-minute voice Q&A costs about $0.20-1.25. A 3-minute live avatar Q&A costs about $0.55-3.30. A pre-rendered 2-minute explainer costs about $0 per view, after a one-off generation cost of about $4 (Veo Lite) to $24 (Veo standard), or about $2-8 via avatar APIs. On a $29/month plan, roughly 10-50 avatar conversations wipe out gross margin. Usage caps or minute packs are needed.
- Falling costs help gross margin but also lower barriers to entry. Any competitor or platform can assemble the same stack (TTS + LLM + avatar API + slide renderer) cheaply, so cost declines do not create a moat.
- Single-vendor dependence is a real risk. The reported Sora 2 API retirement shows a model can be withdrawn within about a year.

### Gaps
- No authoritative historical time series of per-minute prices for AI video or voice. The trend is pieced together from individual price-change announcements and third-party guides.
- The current official OpenAI realtime and ElevenLabs per-minute prices could not be verified on vendor pages (fetch failed). Sources conflict by up to 3x.

## 5. Trends: LinkedIn distribution (external links vs native video and documents)

### Takeaway
The core distribution idea (post a shareable external link on LinkedIn) runs against LinkedIn's ranking. Marketing analyses consistently report external-link posts getting about 40-60% less reach, and organic reach overall falling about 50% (van der Blom). LinkedIn is pushing native video (+36% YoY viewership) and document carousels. The product should produce native LinkedIn assets (MP4 and PDF carousel) that point to the link, rather than relying on the link post itself.

### Cited Findings
- External-link posts get about 60% less reach than identical posts without links, or 40-60% across sources. The "link in first comment" workaround is reportedly also penalized as of early 2026, though sources disagree. LinkedIn has not published definitive guidance (marketing blogs) — [We Change Minds "LinkedIn link tax"](https://www.wechangeminds.com/the-linkedin-link-tax/); [Sendible 2026](https://www.sendible.com/insights/linkedin-algorithm); [Dataslayer Feb 2026](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/linkedin-algorithm-february-2026-whats-working-now)
- Richard van der Blom's annual algorithm report measures an average 50% drop in views and 25% drop in engagement, attributed to LinkedIn reducing viral distribution in favour of relevance (secondary summary) — [Perroly](https://www.perroly.com/blog/linkedin-organic-2026-what-works). The report itself is paid — [van der Blom Gumroad](https://richardvanderblom.gumroad.com/p/olympics-2024-is-in-the-air-here-s-a-meme-to-brighten-up-your-week)
- Format performance (2026 blog data): document/PDF carousels have about 6.6% average engagement and short native video about 5.6% — [Dataslayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/linkedin-algorithm-february-2026-whats-working-now)
- Official: LinkedIn video viewership was +36% YoY (Microsoft FQ2 2025, Oct 2024 to Jan 2025), weekly immersive video views rose 6x QoQ, video uploads were +34% YoY (FQ4 2024), and weekly video creation among publisher-program members was +67% YoY — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/marketing/linkedins-video-push-appears-to-be-working-in-2025/); [Lindsey Gamble](https://lindseygamble.com/blog/linkedin-video-viewership-up-36-year-over-year)
- Metricool 2025: LinkedIn video impressions +73% and video views +52% — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-performance-benchmarks-insights-2025/744819/). Socialinsider: video engagement rate +7% YoY, but average video views per business page fell 36% — [Vidico](https://vidico.com/news/linkedin-video-statistics-australia/)
- Reports say generic AI-generated content is being down-ranked under a 2026 "authenticity" emphasis (single-source, unverified against LinkedIn) — [ZoomSphere](https://www.zoomsphere.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm-2026-why-generic-ai-content-kills-your-organic-reach)

### Inferences
- A "paste content, get a link for LinkedIn" product will lose much of its reach if the link is the post. Exporting a native vertical video clip and/or PDF carousel (which LinkedIn favours), with the interactive link in the profile, featured section or DMs, fits the algorithm better.
- Company pages engage less than personal profiles (1.74% vs 2.6%, section 2). Founder-led personal posting is the channel, which favours selling to founders and individual executives over brand pages.
- Video is LinkedIn's growth format, which supports demand for AI-made explainer videos. But LinkedIn's own creation tools and ad formats could eventually absorb this (not verified; see section 7).

### Gaps
- No official LinkedIn statement quantifying the external-link penalty. All percentages come from third-party marketers.
- No 2026 official LinkedIn video growth figures were found. The latest official numbers are from early 2025.
- The van der Blom 2025/2026 report contents were not directly accessible (paid).

## 6. Trends: buyer and investor behaviour (AI pitch materials, chatbot engagement, AI SDRs, AI-wrapper retention)

### Takeaway
Evidence that investors want to talk to an AI agent about a startup is absent. Investors spend about 2-4 minutes on a deck, and qualitative investor commentary is skeptical of AI-generated materials, though one 2023 blind test found AI-written decks persuasive. Website chat widgets see about 5-10% visitor engagement (weak vendor data). AI agents in sales are a strong enterprise trend, but Gartner warns of hype and cancellations. AI-native apps priced under $50/month show very poor retention (median about 23% gross revenue retention), the biggest structural risk for a solo founder seeking stable MRR.

### Cited Findings
**Investor attention and AI-made pitch materials**
- DocSend: average investor time per deck hit an all-time low of 2:18 in Q4 2022 — [TechCrunch 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/09/22/science-of-pitch-decks/amp). It hit a new low of 2:24 in Q4 2023 after declining throughout 2023 — [PR Newswire via GuruFocus](https://m.gurufocus.com/news/2154228/)
- 2026 figures conflict. A May 2026 promotional release citing DocSend data gives 1:56 total viewing time per seed deck (down 24% since 2021) and describes a market "flooded with AI-generated submissions" — [press release](https://www.myknoxcountynews.com/online_features/press_releases/article_1f0551b9-cec9-5b2e-b8a6-5297b0879e40.html). A secondary site claims 3:44 for seed decks, with 58% read to the last slide — [Peony](https://peony.ink/blog/how-to-track-pitch-deck-engagement)
- Papermark (2025, 3,000 decks): the first page gets more than twice the attention of any other slide, and later pages average about 15 seconds each (secondary) — [Pitchwise](https://www.pitchwise.se/blog/pitch-deck-analytics-investor-interest)
- Clarify Capital (2023; 250 investors + 250 business owners, blind): 80% found GPT-4 decks convincing vs 39% for human decks. The study was run by a commercial lender and is dated — [Clarify Capital](https://clarifycapital.com/the-future-of-investment-pitching)
- Investor commentary: an AI-generated deck signals "I outsourced my thinking to AI" (single investor's opinion) — [Searchfunder](https://searchfunder.com/post/investor-perspective-ai-generated-pitch-decks-really-hurt-your-credibility). Investors increasingly use AI themselves to summarize decks and data rooms but still rely on founder trust and live thinking — [MEAN CEO 2026](https://blog.mean.ceo/?p=4223)

**Website chatbot engagement (weak, vendor-sourced)**
- A workable sitewide chat-widget engagement rate is about 5-10% of eligible visitors, and 10%+ on high-intent pages (vendor guide) — [TRTC](https://trtc.io/blog/details/chat-widget-conversion-rate-v2). Tidio retail case study: 8.99% bot engagement rate — [Tidio](https://www.tidio.com/blog/chatbot-statistics/)
- About 58-60% of B2B companies use chatbots, vs 42% of B2C (vendor roundups, no methodology shown) — [Tidio](https://www.tidio.com/blog/chatbot-statistics/); [Litslink](https://litslink.com/blog/chatbot-statistics)
- Claims of "2.8x conversion" or "10-40% lift" from chat widgets are attributed to unnamed providers and should be discounted — [TRTC](https://trtc.io/blog/details/chat-widget-conversion-rate-v2)

**AI agents / AI SDRs in sales**
- Gartner (26 Aug 2025 press release, via secondary): 40% of enterprise apps will feature task-specific AI agents by end-2026, up from under 5% in 2025. Gartner also predicts more than 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end-2027, and estimates only about 130 of the vendors claiming "agentic AI" are real ("agent washing") — [Sales POP](https://salespop.net/sales-technology/what-b2b-sales-leaders-need-to-know-about-ai-agents-in-sales-2026/)
- Gartner (secondary, unverified): by 2028 AI agents will outnumber human sellers 10:1, and fewer than 40% of sellers say AI agents improved their productivity — [Cortinovis](https://www.cortinovis.de/?p=67359)
- Salesforce State of Sales 2026 names AI agents as the #1 growth strategy for sales organizations (secondary) — [Ciela](https://ciela.ai/blogs/ai-agent-adoption-statistics-2026)

**AI-wrapper saturation and retention**
- ChartMogul "The AI churn wave" (Kyle Poyar, Dec 2025) covers about 2,700 B2B SaaS, 600 B2C SaaS and 200 AI-native companies with at least $250k ARR — [ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-retention-the-ai-churn-wave/). Secondary coverage gives AI-native median gross revenue retention of about 40%, falling to about 23% for products under $50/month — [Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/customer-churn/). The search extract also reported a B2B SaaS median of 82% (labelled NRR) with an upper quartile of 97%; the metric label could not be confirmed.

### Inferences
- Investors are the weakest buyer persona. They spend minutes per deck, use their own AI to summarize, and some view AI-made materials as a negative signal. An investor-facing "chat with my startup" agent has no supporting engagement data and may hurt credibility. Pitching the product as a founder fundraising tool is riskier than as a B2B sales or marketing asset.
- Website chat engagement of about 5-10% means most visitors to a shared link will watch or skim and never ask a question. The video/deck has to carry the value, and the Q&A agent is an add-on.
- The AI-SDR / sales-agent trend helps a B2B "interactive sales room" positioning. But the market is crowded with "agent-washed" vendors, and Gartner expects many projects to be cancelled.
- The ChartMogul retention data is the strongest quantitative warning: a sub-$50/month AI tool keeps only about a quarter of revenue after a year (median). Stable MRR requires either a recurring use case (ongoing sales/marketing, multiple links per month, analytics and lead capture) or higher-priced team plans.

### Gaps
- No data on whether investors (VCs, angels) engage with AI chat agents on startup pages. This is a key unknown for the investor use case.
- No rigorous, non-vendor study of B2B website chatbot engagement-to-conversion. Drift/Qualified primary data was not found.
- No published data on how investors react to AI-generated video explainers specifically (as opposed to decks).
- The ChartMogul figures were not read directly. The 40% and 23% numbers come from secondary coverage.

## 7. Is the market growing, consolidating or being commoditized by platforms?

### Takeaway
All three are happening. Spend is growing fast (enterprise GenAI 3.2x in 2025, more than half of US businesses paying for AI). Value is concentrating in a few scaled, well-funded leaders (Gamma $2.1B, Synthesia about $4B, HeyGen about $95M ARR). The core features of "content to narrated video/deck" are being bundled for free or cheaply into Google NotebookLM, Microsoft 365 Copilot and Canva. A standalone solo-founder product has to win on a narrow workflow (e.g., B2B sales follow-up rooms with lead capture and analytics), not on generation quality.

### Cited Findings
- Growth: enterprise GenAI spend reached $37B in 2025 (3.2x YoY), with $19B at the application layer — [Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/). More than half of US businesses pay for AI tools (Ramp, Spring 2026) — [Ramp](https://assets.ramp.com/nextjs/files/spring-2026-business-spending-report.pdf)
- Concentration in leaders: Gamma about $100M ARR, profitable, $2.1B valuation (Nov 2025) — [Sacra Gamma](https://sacra.com/c/gamma/). Synthesia about $146M ARR (Sep 2025), reported $4B valuation (Jan 2026) — [Sacra Synthesia](https://sacra.com/c/synthesia). HeyGen about $95M ARR (Sep 2025) — [Sacra HeyGen](https://sacra.com/c/heygen). Delphi (AI clone Q&A) raised $16M from Tier-1 VCs (June 2025) — [VCBacked](https://www.vcbacked.co/company/delphi)
- Google NotebookLM: Video Overviews (mid-2025) turn a user's own documents into narrated slideshow videos, in 80+ languages — [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/7/google-is-introducing-video-overviews-in-notebooklm); [LeadWithAI](https://www.leadwithai.co/article/generate-how-to-videos-with-notebooklms-video-overviews). "Cinematic Video Overviews" (4 Mar 2026) add scenes and continuity, initially for AI Ultra subscribers and English only — [OnlineMarketing.de](https://onlinemarketing.de/technologie/google-cinematic-video-overviews-notebooklm); [WWWhatsNew](https://wwwhatsnew.com/?p=488468)
- Microsoft: Copilot slide generation from natural-language prompts began rolling out across Microsoft 365 Copilot and PowerPoint Web in early 2026. In July 2026 Copilot in SharePoint gained direct generation of PowerPoint files from SharePoint content. Google's Gemini is adding a Canva integration — [BornCity](https://borncity.com/news/copilot-gemini-microsoft-und-google-ruesten-ki-praesentationen-auf/); [ChatSlide](https://www.chatslide.ai/articles/ai-powered-slide-design-trends-2026)
- Canva: a third-party 2026 comparison cites more than 150M users, with presentations the #2 content type. A July 2026 review argues that for Canva and Google Slides users, built-in AI is "good enough" that a separate app is hard to justify — [Lumichats](https://lumichats.com/blog/best-ai-for-presentations-slides-2026); [is4.ai](https://is4.ai/blog/our-blog-1/top-10-ai-presentation-tools-2026-comparison-420)
- Agent washing: Gartner estimates only about 130 genuine agentic-AI vendors among the many claiming it — [Sales POP](https://salespop.net/sales-technology/what-b2b-sales-leaders-need-to-know-about-ai-agents-in-sales-2026/)

### Inferences
- **Growing:** yes. Category spend and willingness to pay for AI content tools are clearly rising.
- **Consolidating:** yes, at the top. Gamma (decks and links), Synthesia/HeyGen (avatar video, now adding interactive avatars), DocSend (share links and analytics, owned by Dropbox), Storylane/Navattic/Arcade (interactive demos) and Delphi (AI clone Q&A) each own one piece of this bundle and have far more distribution and capital than a solo founder.
- **Commoditized:** "turn my documents into a narrated explainer video/deck" is becoming a free feature (NotebookLM Video Overviews, Copilot, Canva, Gemini). The remaining differentiation is workflow: a shareable branded link, viewer analytics, lead capture and CRM sync, a Q&A agent grounded in company content, and LinkedIn-native exports. That is close to the "digital sales room" category, where established vendors already compete.
- Net for a solo founder targeting MRR: there is enough market to support a $10-50k MRR niche business. But differentiation through generation quality alone will not survive platform bundling, and the investor/fundraising use case is the weakest (shrinking deal counts, episodic usage, skeptical buyers). A B2B sales/marketing-room positioning with usage-priced Q&A minutes is better supported by the trend data.

### Gaps
- No confirmed information on LinkedIn building native AI explainer or Q&A features for company pages or profiles. This is a material unknown, since LinkedIn controls the main distribution channel.
- No data on Canva's or Google Vids' 2026 AI video/avatar launches. Search results did not surface primary announcements.
- No M&A data for the "digital sales room" / interactive demo category in 2025-2026 was retrieved.
