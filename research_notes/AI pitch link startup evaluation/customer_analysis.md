# Customer Analysis: Who Pays for an "AI Explainer Link" (AI video/deck + voice/chat Q&A agent), as of October 2026

How this was researched: the egress proxy blocked WebFetch for every site tried (gartner.com, digitalcommerce360.com, hummingdeck.com, news.ycombinator.com and others returned DNS/403 errors). The web-search tool rejected reddit.com outright, and the shared web-search budget ran out before X/LinkedIn could be searched. So every finding below comes from search-engine result summaries, not full-page reads. Quotes are as the search tool returned them and have not been checked against the original pages. Data older than 2025 is flagged with its date.

## Q1. Candidate buyer segments and their pain: which segment has the most acute, recurring, paid pain?

### Takeaway
Fundraising founders have the most acute pain, but it is episodic: a raise lasts about 3–6 months, and the next one comes about 2 years later. Their audience (VCs) is also the most hostile to AI-made materials. B2B startups and agencies explaining their offering to buyers have a weaker but continuous pain, and buyer behavior already favors self-serve research, so this is where recurring, paid use is most plausible. Job seekers have the weakest case. Accelerators and real estate are possible channels or verticals, but I found little direct evidence for them.

### Cited Findings
**Segment 1: Pre-seed/seed founders fundraising (acute, episodic)**
- Fundraising takes a lot of outreach. A 2020 DocSend pre-seed report (174 startups, 2019 data) found that some founders raise with little outreach, while "a whole other group needs to contact upwards of 100, 150 or even 200 investors to close a pre-seed round" (DATED: 2020). — [TechCrunch, Mar 2020](https://techcrunch.com/2020/03/03/docsends-new-pre-seed-data-shows-how-many-founders-you-should-have-and-how-many-investors-you-should-meet/)
- A later DocSend pre-seed report reported an average of 71 investors contacted and 46 meetings secured. The search summary did not give its exact date. — [DocSend blog: pre-seed fundraising report](https://www.docsend.com/blog/pre-seed-fundraising-report/)
- DocSend's 2024 analysis of 400+ pre-seed and seed startups found that pre-seed rounds averaged 12 weeks (DATED: 2024). — [StockTitan / Dropbox DocSend release](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/DBX/v-cs-prioritize-people-in-an-ai-heavy-landscape-according-to-new-z3rqvcox6orq.html)
- 2026 guides say a seed round typically takes 3–6 months from first investor meeting to money in the bank, with US companies trending toward 3–4 months. — [SeedForge, 2026](https://seedforge.com/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-close-a-seed-round-in-2026)
- Carta's Feb 2026 analysis of 9,843 US rounds puts the median Seed-to-Series A gap at 1.9 years in Q4 2025, shrinking back toward the "traditional 18–24 month window". — [Carta Data](https://carta.com/data/linkedin-time-between-startup-rounds-improving/)
- A secondary summary of Carta data gives a median seed-to-Series A interval of 616 days in Q2 2025. — [WaveUp seed benchmarks 2026](https://waveup.com/blog/seed-round-benchmarks/)
- Market volume: Carta's Q2 2026 pre-seed data covers 11,546 transactions (SAFEs and notes) totaling $3.19B in that quarter. — [Carta, State of Pre-Seed Q2 2026](https://carta.com/data/state-of-pre-seed-q2-2026/)
- Q2 2026 seed: per a Carta LinkedIn post reported by TipRanks, seed capital raised was up 37% while the number of seed rounds was down 20%. That means fewer companies are raising, with bigger checks. — [TipRanks](https://www.tipranks.com/news/private-companies/carta-data-points-to-larger-venture-rounds-and-tighter-selection-in-q2-2026)

**Segment 2: Early-stage B2B startups doing sales/outbound (continuous)**
- Gartner (June 2025): 61% of B2B buyers prefer an overall rep-free buying experience (632 buyers, surveyed Aug–Sep 2024), and 73% actively avoid suppliers that send irrelevant outreach. — [Gartner press release, Jun 25 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience); [Demand Gen Report](https://www.demandgenreport.com/industry-news/news-brief/3-out-of-5-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience-gartner/49802/)
- A follow-up Gartner survey (646 buyers, Aug–Sep 2025) put the rep-free preference at 67%. Buyers still prefer seller input for "contextual" tasks such as deciding whether a product fits their company. — [Digital Commerce 360, Mar 17 2026](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/03/17/gartner-b2b-buyers-rep-free-purchasing-ai-reshapes-sales/)
- Vidyard (April 2025 survey of 656 sales and marketing leaders): 92% say video is an essential part of their revenue strategy. — [Vidyard: Future of Revenue](https://www.vidyard.com/future-revenue-strategy-agentic-ai-and-video/)

**Segment 3: SMBs/agencies explaining services (continuous)**
- Qwilr, a proposal tool positioned for "agencies, small to mid-sized teams, and consultants", charges $35/user/month on annual billing ($39 monthly). Agencies already pay per seat for interactive, explain-and-close documents. — [Verdocs: Qwilr pricing 2026](https://verdocs.com/blog/qwilr-pricing); [ProposalFlowLab](https://proposalflowlab.com/pricing/qwilr-pricing)
- Storydoc (interactive decks with AI) lists Starter at about $19.80/month and Pro at about $36/month on annual billing. Team pricing requires contacting sales. — [AnyGen Storydoc review 2026](https://www.anygen.io/showcase/storydoc-review/index.html)

**Segment 4: Solo consultants, coaches and creators (continuous, low budgets)**
- Delphi, an AI "digital mind" clone with chat and voice that coaches and creators use, has a free tier and paid tiers that reviews most often list as Builder at about $79/month (annual) and Scaler at about $299/month. Other sources say $99–$349/month. Named users include Tiago Forte, Dr. Mark Hyman and Brian Halligan. — [Creator Economy Tools](https://creatoreconomytools.com/tool/delphi); [ToolDirectory](https://tooldirectory.ai/tools/delphi); [Personify AI clone cost guide](https://personify.fyi/blog/ai-clone-cost/)
- A competitor-written review says Delphi's realized price per subscriber "tends to land on the low side". It comes from a rival vendor, so the bias is likely. — [David Riha, Delphi alternatives 2026](https://davidriha.com/blog/best-delphi-alternatives-2026/)

**Segment 5: Job seekers and personal brands (episodic, low willingness to pay)**
- Employers increasingly screen with AI: one roundup says 72% of companies use AI in recruiting (up from 58% in 2024) and 83% used AI to review resumes in 2025. — [The Interview Guys](https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/83-of-companies-will-use-ai-resume-screening-by-2025-despite-67-acknowledging-bias-concerns/)
- Greenhouse's 2026 report, as summarized by InterviewFlow, says 63% of US candidates have experienced an AI interview. — [InterviewFlow AI](https://interviewflowai.com/blog/ai-recruitment-statistics)

**Segment 6: Accelerators, incubators, VCs and demo days**
- Accelerator software spend goes mainly to management platforms (applications, cohorts, demo day, portfolio reporting) such as AcceleratorApp, Babele, Dealum and Visible. Search found no portfolio-wide "pitch link" licenses. — [Innoloft accelerator software 2026](https://innoloft.com/en-us/blog/accelerator-software); [AcceleratorApp](https://www.acceleratorapp.co/en/blogs/category/all/blog/best-accelerator-management-software/); [Pory](https://www.pory.io/use-cases/accelerators)
- Accelerators increasingly compete on "cloud credits, AI credits, software perks" rather than mentorship alone. That suggests a perks or partner-distribution channel rather than paid licenses. — [Credit for Startups](https://creditforstartups.com/resources/startup-accelerator-programs)

**Segment 7: Verticals (real estate)**
- NAR's 2026 technology report says AI use has become routine (weekly or daily) for nearly half of Realtors. — [NAR, 2026](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/technology/the-tech-real-estate-agents-rely-on-most-in-2026); [WRE News](https://wrenews.com/nar-2026-technology-report-realtors-ai-use/)
- A vendor citing NAR's Member Profile puts professional listing video at about $350–$750 per property. One AI listing-video vendor charges $599/month for teams listing 10+ properties a month. — [Reel-E real estate video cost 2026](https://www.reel-e.ai/blog/real-estate-video-cost)
- Per a vendor citing NAR, only 12% of home sellers say their agent used video to market their home. — [Reel-E marketing statistics](https://www.reel-e.ai/blog/real-estate-marketing-statistics)

### Inferences
- **Most acute pain: fundraising founders.** A founder sends the same story to 50–200 investors under existential pressure. But the job only exists during a raise of about 3–6 months, repeated roughly every 2 years. That is the profile of a tool people rent and then cancel.
- **Most recurring and most paid pain: B2B startups and SMBs/agencies.** They explain their offering to prospects every week. Buyers already prefer rep-free, self-serve research (61–67%), and these customers already pay $18–$40 per seat per month for adjacent tools (Loom, Qwilr, Storydoc, Chatbase; see Q5).
- **Agencies have a structural advantage.** One agency can create links for many end clients, which gives multi-seat or white-label recurring revenue and lowers churn compared with a single founder.
- **Real estate has a recurring per-listing job and a known budget**, about $350–$750 for a professional listing video. But it is a separate go-to-market, already targeted by HeyGen and vertical tools, and a poor fit for a "founder/company" product.
- **Job seekers are the weakest segment.** Their need is episodic, their budgets are low, and the "viewers" (recruiters) increasingly screen with AI rather than watch media.
- **Accelerators are more likely a distribution channel** (a perk bundled per cohort) than an early revenue line.

### Gaps
- I found no primary survey that measures pain intensity per segment for "explaining what my company does". The segment pain ranking above is inferred from adjacent behavioral and pricing data.
- No data on accelerator or VC budgets for portfolio-wide tool licenses, or any example of one buying a pitch or explainer tool for all portfolio companies.
- No data on recruiting firms, or on whether recruiters watch video resumes. A TieTalent article on the data behind video resumes ([link](https://tietalent.com/en/blog/250/are-video-resumes-the-answer-what-the-data-says)) appeared in search, but its content was not retrievable.
- A Carta Q2 2026 absolute count of seed rounds was not found, only the -20% change.

## Q2. Evidence of demand: what founders say about explaining their startup, explainer videos, sending decks, and AI pitch tools

### Takeaway
Founders clearly struggle with getting decks read and getting feedback, and narrated or async decks are recommended practice. But the public, searchable evidence I could reach shows demand for AI tools that help founders prepare or analyze pitches, not for AI that stands in for the founder. A builder of a closely analogous AI explainer product said publicly that the use is low-frequency with high churn risk.

### Cited Findings
- On Indie Hackers, a founder building AI comic-strip explainers for SaaS products flagged that the core feature has low frequency, with "high churn risk after they finish their explainer". They were weighing add-on features to fix this. This is the closest public analog to the proposed product. — [Indie Hackers: "Pivoting my SaaS MVP away from AI images…"](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/pivoting-my-saas-mvp-away-from-ai-images-feedback-wanted-on-planned-acquisition-vs-retention-strategy-BIPxmQ52P6FB2rzfawXo)
- A fundraising playbook on Indie Hackers by the Supademo founder recommends fewer than 15 slides at pre-seed and "recording a short 5-min narrated version" of the deck to speed up investor conversations. This is evidence that async narrated decks are an established practice. — [Indie Hackers: "A Step-by-Step Playbook to Raising in a Down Market"](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/a-step-by-step-playbook-to-raising-in-a-down-market-4e0b080831)
- Gatekeep, an Indie Hackers launch, lets founders pitch an AI persona of an investor and get scores. About 63 days after launch it reported "30+ founders have pitched", which shows modest early pull for AI pitch-prep tools. — [Indie Hackers: Gatekeep](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/gatekeep)
- An Indie Hackers founder writing about a first pitch reported that the investor liked the idea but was "not clear on how the implementation is unique versus competitors". This is a common explanation gap. — [Indie Hackers: First Pitch Deck and Pitch to Investor](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/tipalink/first-pitch-deck-and-pitch-to-investor--Lrdsg-axAiWXwtXFzE2)
- A Substack post aimed at founders is titled "Founders: are you fed-up of sending out your deck and receiving no response or feedback?". The title itself signals the pain. — [alexfmac Substack](https://alexfmac.substack.com/p/investment-for-your-startup)
- TechCrunch notes that it is relatively uncommon for founders to get candid feedback on how they communicated the problem, solution and path to success. — [TechCrunch video: VCs offer live feedback on pitch decks](https://techcrunch.com/video/vcs-offer-live-feedback-on-audience-members-startup-pitch-decks)
- SaaStr's AI VC deck analyzer processed 700+ pitch decks soon after launch. SaaStr's headline is that "few are fundable", which shows demand for free AI pitch feedback. — [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/what-weve-learned-from-our-first-700-vc-pitch-decks-in-saastrs-new-ai-pitch-analyzer-the-data-is-brutal-and-eye-opening/)
- Harvard Innovation Labs publishes a guide to building a VC voice agent in ElevenLabs to rehearse pitch meetings, grounded in a specific partner's public writing. This shows founders using voice agents to practice, not to stand in for themselves. — [Harvard Innovation Labs](https://innovationlabs.harvard.edu/how-to/accelerate-your-startups-vc-fundraising-with-ai)
- AskSila sells "digital twins trained on real experience from founders", focused on fundraising, pitch decks and investor conversations, as educational guidance. It is an adjacent "talk to a founder's AI" product aimed at learners, not investors. — [AskSila](https://www.asksila.com/campaign/ask-a-founder)
- A founder study of AI-generated decks concluded they "give structure but not strategy". — [Whitepage Studio: Why AI-Generated Pitch Decks Fail (25 founders)](https://www.whitepage.studio/blog/why-ai-generated-pitch-decks-fail)
- Custom explainer videos from freelancers on Dribbble cost about $200–$2,000, which is the cost a founder avoids by not recording or commissioning a video. — [Dribbble: Product Explainer Video](https://dribbble.com/services/95938-Product-Explainer-Video)
- An older TechCrunch piece argued that most companies are better off converting existing site visitors than spending scarce resources on explainer-video production (DATED, older article). — [TechCrunch: The Rise Of The Explainer Video](https://techcrunch.com/?p=525066)

### Inferences
- The pain founders state publicly is "my deck doesn't get read or answered" and "investors don't get what's unique". The proposed product partly addresses this, but the evidence points to clarity of the story as the binding constraint, not the format.
- Existing traction in AI pitch tools sits in founder-side preparation (scoring, rehearsal, analysis), where the founder stays in control. "AI speaks for me to investors" has no visible organic demand signal in what I could access.
- The Indie Hackers explainer founder's own churn warning is the most direct evidence on retention for this exact product shape: one explainer gets made, then the customer leaves.

### Gaps
- **Reddit (r/startups, r/SaaS, r/Entrepreneur, r/founder) could not be searched.** The search tool returned an error that reddit.com is not accessible to its user agent, so this note has no Reddit quotes.
- **X/LinkedIn founder and VC posts were not collected** because the shared web-search budget ran out mid-research.
- Hacker News "Show HN" posts about AI pitch agents were not found by search, and HN pages could not be fetched.
- None of the quotes above were checked against the full page, because WebFetch was blocked.

## Q3. The viewer/investor side: how investors consume pitch materials, and whether they'd use or distrust an AI agent answering for a founder

### Takeaway
Investors give decks only about 2–4 minutes, so short async formats fit how they behave. But public VC commentary through 2026 is consistently negative on AI-generated pitch content. Investors prize the founder's own thinking and live, unscripted Q&A. I found no survey or public VC statement endorsing an AI agent that answers on a founder's behalf, and several statements imply it would be read as a negative signal.

### Cited Findings
**How long investors look at decks**
- The most-cited figure, 3 min 44 s per seed deck, traces back to DocSend's 2015 fundraising study (DATED: 2015). A 2026 summary says DocSend's seed guide (updated March 2026) still reports 3:44 with a 58% completion rate, and the pre-seed guide (updated Feb 2026) reports 4:10. — [Hummingdeck, Pitch Deck Statistics 2026](https://hummingdeck.com/blog/pitch-deck-benchmarks-2026); [Presentations.ai](https://www.presentations.ai/blog/pitch-deck-statistics)
- From 2022 to 2023, VCs spent almost 20% less time reviewing pitch decks: 2 min 12 s versus 2 min 42 s a year earlier (DATED: 2023). — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2592727)
- Search summaries also report Papermark data of 3.2 minutes per complete-deck review (Jan–Dec 2024), surfaced via secondary roundups and not verified against Papermark. — [Pynn](https://pynn.ai/blog/what-investors-read-in-your-deck-and-how-long-you-have); [PitchGrade](https://pitchgrade.com/blog/what-investors-read-pitch-deck-docsend-data)
- DocSend Q1 interest data showed investor engagement waning amid macro uncertainty (DATED: 2022). — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/docsend-q1-pitch-deck-interest-data-shows-investor-engagement-waning-amidst-macroeconomic-uncertainty-301519498.html)

**VC commentary on AI-generated pitch materials**
- An early-stage VC in Fast Company: decks built with AI "sound exactly like everyone else". The author supports AI for research and polishing but objects to AI "doing the thinking". — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91591431/ai-generated-pitch-decks)
- An investor on Searchfunder says an AI-generated deck signals "I outsourced my thinking to AI", and that "You will get this stigma even if your deck just *looks* AI-generated." — [Searchfunder](https://searchfunder.com/post/investor-perspective-ai-generated-pitch-decks-really-hurt-your-credibility)
- Monday Morning newsletter (March 2026), "AI Is the Worst Thing to Happen to Early Stage Investors": AI-generated narratives hold together with no typos, which is exactly the problem. "The in-person test still works, but only in a room, with follow-up questions, in real time." — [Monday Morning Substack](https://mondaymorning.substack.com/p/ai-is-the-worst-thing-to-happen-to)
- Mayfield's Navin Chaddha (via The Venture Lens) accepts AI-polished decks if founders edit and own them, and sees *not* using AI as a warning sign. Patrick Mebus (techtrust.ai) argues the deck itself is becoming obsolete once AI makes polished slides trivial to produce. — [The Venture Lens: How Founders and VCs use AI](https://theventurelens.substack.com/p/ai-founders-vc)
- Precursor Ventures' Charles Hudson warns that AI decks risk sameness and says investors spend the most time on the team slide and key narrative. — [The Venture Lens: Pitch Decks in the AI Era](https://theventurelens.substack.com/p/ai-pitch-deck-tips)
- Search summaries report that VCs at firms such as EQT and Moonfire use AI to pre-screen decks before a partner opens the file. This came from the same Venture Lens and related coverage and was not verified on the full page. — [The Venture Lens](https://theventurelens.substack.com/p/ai-founders-vc); [V7 Labs: AI for Pitch Deck Analysis (2026)](https://www.v7labs.com/blog/ai-pitch-deck-analysis)
- Search summaries report a TechCrunch panel quip that "the more a founder says AI in the pitch, the less AI the company likely uses". The source appears to be TechCrunch's Dec 29, 2025 article, whose text I could not read. — [TechCrunch, Dec 29 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/29/vcs-spill-what-they-really-want-to-hear-in-a-founder-pitch)
- Zerodha CEO Nithin Kamath (Aug 2026): AI is now "table stakes" in a startup pitch, not a differentiator. — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/artificial-intelligence/story/ai-is-no-longer-a-startup-pitch-differentiator-says-zerodha-ceo-nithin-kamath-549595-2026-08-17)

**Video, async formats and teasers**
- At TechCrunch Disrupt 2022, VC Jomayra Herrera suggested sending "almost like a teaser version of the deck with enough data and information to give us a sense of where you are" (DATED: 2022). — [TechCrunch, Oct 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/10/27/3-vcs-explain-how-founders-can-stand-out-when-pitching/)
- Alex Iskold argued in 2016 that pitch video should replace the deck (DATED: 2016; no evidence it became standard). — [Startup Hacks](https://www.startuphacks.vc/blog/2016/09/27/lets-face-it-it-is-time-to-upgrade-the-pitch-deck-to-pitch-video)
- Video-production vendor blogs claim more VCs request a short pitch video, and one cites "research" that a pitch video lifts intro-to-meeting conversion 1.7x. The figure could not be traced to a named study, and the advice in these sources is that video should support the deck and meeting, not replace them. — [Pixel8 Production](https://www.pixel8production.com/blog/investor-pitch-video-production); [LV Prod](https://www.lv-prod.com/blog/investor-pitch-video-what-vcs-actually-want-to-see/)

**Trust and deepfake risk**
- A vendor-authored guide notes a federal indictment in which a founder allegedly staged video calls with AI-generated executives and board members to extract venture money. Investor impersonation is a documented scam vector. — [Lawyerd](https://lawyerd.net/blog/fake-vc-investor-impersonation-deepfake-scam/)
- A HeyGen-focused blog (Oct 2026) acknowledges that a slightly wrong smile, gaze, cadence or timing in avatar video "can create distrust". — [Mean CEO: HeyGen News](https://blog.mean.ceo/heygen-news-october-2026/)

### Inferences
- An investor gives a deck 2–4 minutes. A 60–90 second explainer plus an optional Q&A box fits that attention budget better than a long video.
- For the investor audience, two parts of the product are liabilities: the AI avatar or synthetic voice, and an agent that "answers for" the founder. They trigger the exact sameness and outsourced-thinking stigma VCs describe, plus deepfake suspicion. If the founder segment is pursued, the safer design is the founder's real voice or real slides, with an agent clearly labelled as a deck assistant that cites the founder's source material.
- Investors already use AI to screen decks. The investor-side "agent" is therefore likely to be the VC's own tool (ChatGPT or an internal screener) reading the founder's content, not the founder's chatbot. This weakens the case for a founder-hosted Q&A agent and strengthens the case for clean, machine-readable source material.

### Gaps
- **No survey of VCs on AI agents answering on a founder's behalf**, and no on-record partner comment about AI-avatar pitch videos, was found.
- No primary DocSend 2025 or 2026 report could be verified. A search summary also mentioned a secondary analysis claiming DocSend's live tracker has no data after September 2024, and I could not identify or verify that source. The recency of the "3:44" benchmark is therefore uncertain.
- No data on what share of VCs watch founder videos when they are included.

## Q4. Do website visitors and B2B buyers engage with AI chat or voice agents? Engagement, conversion and self-serve preferences

### Takeaway
Buyers clearly want self-serve research and increasingly use AI chatbots for it. But they mostly use third-party assistants (ChatGPT and similar), not vendors' own bots, and they keep strong preferences for human access and against intrusive widgets. Engagement benchmarks for on-site chatbots are mostly vendor-sourced and imply that only a minority of visitors interact.

### Cited Findings
- G2's "Answer Economy" research: 51% of B2B software buyers now start their research with an AI chatbot more often than with Google, up from 29% in G2's April 2025 Buyer Behavior Report. 69% chose a different vendor than planned because of chatbot guidance, and about a third bought from a vendor they had never heard of. A secondary summary says it was based on a March 2026 survey of 1,076 buyers. — [PR Newswire / G2](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/new-g2-research-half-of-b2b-software-buyers-now-start-their-research-with-ai-chatbots-302742807.html); [Demand Gen Report](https://www.demandgenreport.com/industry-news/news-brief/half-of-b2b-software-buyers-now-start-their-research-with-ai-chatbots-g2/52737/); [G2 company news](https://company.g2.com/news/g2-research-the-answer-economy)
- Gartner (2026, 3,566 customers): customers are 3x more likely to use third-party GenAI such as ChatGPT than a company's own chatbot for support, and 87% say companies using GenAI in service must preserve access to a human. — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/gartner-customers-third-party-genai-over-company-chatbots); [Valasys](https://valasys.com/gartner-ai-customer-support-human-access/); [CMSWire](https://www.cmswire.com/customer-experience/gartner-genai-findings-suggest-customers-dont-care-about-your-ai/)
- Gartner's earlier survey (5,728 customers, dated 2024; sources disagree on Dec 2023 vs. Jul 2024): 64% would prefer companies didn't use AI in service, 60% worry AI makes it harder to reach a human, and 42% don't trust AI-generated answers (DATED: 2023/2024). — [Getmacha customer trust stats](https://www.getmacha.com/blog/customer-trust-ai-customer-service-statistics); [Outsource Accelerator](https://news.outsourceaccelerator.com/human-customer-service-gartner/)
- Gartner prediction (Aug 2025): by 2030, 75% of B2B buyers will prefer sales experiences that prioritize human interaction over AI. This is a forecast, not a measured stat. — [Gartner press release, Aug 25 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-08-25-gartner-says-by-2030-that-75-percent-of-b2b-buyers-will-prefer-sales-experiences-that-prioritize-human-interaction-over-ai)
- SurveyMonkey (Dec 2025, 2,017 Americans): 79% strongly prefer speaking with a human over an AI agent. Five9 (Oct 2024, 4,000 US/UK consumers): 75% prefer a real human. — [SurveyMonkey](https://www.surveymonkey.com/curiosity/customer-service-statistics/); [Getmacha](https://www.getmacha.com/blog/customer-trust-ai-customer-service-statistics)
- Chatbot conversion benchmarks are vendor-sourced and wide-ranging. ZoomInfo's guidance: 10–20% visitor-to-meeting for meeting-booking bots on high-intent pages, 3–8% for general qualification bots. — [ZoomInfo Pipeline](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/marketing/chatbot-metrics)
- Wonderchat (vendor) claims conversational AI converts at 15–25% and cites Unbounce's 3.8% median landing-page conversion for B2B SaaS (6.6% cross-industry). — [Wonderchat B2B Website Conversion Benchmark 2026](https://wonderchat.io/blog/b2b-website-conversion-report-2026)
- Docket models B2B chat engagement at 18% of visitors as an illustrative assumption, not a measured benchmark. — [Docket](https://www.docket.io/blog/chatbot-b2b-use-cases)
- Hacker News sentiment: commenters despise auto-opening "Can I help you" pop-ups, with one blocking Intercom-style widgets with injected CSS (2020). — [HN item 24834779](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=24834779)
- In a 2022 thread, commenters said "the problem isn't live chat, it's *nagging*". An opt-in chat button that reaches a human works well; one quote reads "The chatbot should never pop up on its own. At most, there should be a 'talk to our chatbot' button." — [HN item 33905175](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=33905175); [HN item 33904017](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=33904017)
- "No one wants to talk to your chatbot" was a 2023 HN thread (DATED: 2023). — [HN item 36899656](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36899656)
- A recent HN thread, "All my clients wanted a carousel, now it's an AI chatbot", got about 112 points and 51 comments according to a secondary write-up. It frames on-site AI chatbots as a client-driven fad, like carousels. — [HN item 48072720](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48072720); [PromptZone write-up](https://www.promptzone.com/lin_korhonen/ai-chatbots-dethrone-carousels-4m5)
- One commenter (in the 2025-era thread above, per search summary) said they abandoned a farm-software trial because "every page had a chatbot window that hovered in the bottom middle and could not be dismissed". — [HN item 48072720](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48072720)

**Video consumption by B2B buyers**
- Completion rates attributed to Vidyard and Wistia benchmarks: videos under 1 minute reach about 65% completion, versus about 20% for videos over 20 minutes. Vidyard's 2025 benchmark analyzed about 1M videos and covers adoption of its AI Avatars. — [Vidyard Benchmark Report](https://www.vidyard.com/business-video-benchmarks/); [Vidico B2B video stats 2026](https://vidico.com/news/b2b-video-marketing-statistics/)
- Personalized video in outbound: Loom reports Intercom raised email reply rates 19% with Loom videos. Sendspark (vendor) claims a 200–300% lift in response rates. Both are vendor claims. — [Loom sales use case](https://www.loom.com/use-case/sales); [Sendspark](https://blog.sendspark.com/personalized-video)

**Distribution channel (the "post it on LinkedIn" premise)**
- LinkedIn link posts get less reach. Studies range from about 18.8% lower reach (van der Blom, 1.3M posts) to 26.5% and 36%, with 60% claimed at the upper end. MagicPost's sample of 566,957 posts through June 2026 found a link preview card roughly halved median impressions. LinkedIn has not confirmed a deliberate penalty. — [Ordinal link-penalty study](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/linkedin-link-penalty-study); [MagicPost](https://magicpost.in/blog/linkedin-external-links-reach); [Feedboss](https://www.feedboss.ai/blog/do-linkedin-links-reduce-reach); [Linkedotter, Jun 2026](https://www.linkedotter.com/articles/linkedin-llm-algorithm-external-link-penalty-b2b-june-2026)

### Inferences
- The viewer-side job is real ("let me understand this company without a sales call"). But buyers increasingly do it in their own AI assistant, which competes with a vendor-hosted agent for the same visit. The product's Q&A agent will likely be used by a minority of visitors (plausibly single-digit to low-double-digit percentages if the vendor benchmarks hold). It should be opt-in, never a pop-up, and always offer a "talk to the human" path such as Calendly or email.
- A short video (under 1 minute) is more likely to be watched than a long one. A voice agent adds friction that I found no evidence buyers want.
- LinkedIn reach falls when a post carries an external link, which directly undercuts "share the link on LinkedIn" as a growth loop. Native video or document posts may outperform the link.

### Gaps
- **No engagement or conversion data was found for voice agents** embedded on websites or links.
- No independent (non-vendor) benchmark for on-site chatbot engagement rate.
- A widely repeated 6sense 2025 claim, that buyers complete about 61% of their evaluation before contacting a vendor, could only be found in secondary sources and is not used above. Examples: [Corporate Visions](https://corporatevisions.com/blog/b2b-buying-behavior-statistics-trends/) and [Omnibound](https://www.omnibound.ai/blog/b2b-buying-statistics).

## Q5. Willingness to pay: what these segments pay for adjacent tools, whether the need is episodic or continuous, and what that implies for churn

### Takeaway
The adjacent price anchors are low: about $10–$65 per user per month for decks, data rooms, async video, avatars and proposals, with chatbots at $40–$150 and AI clones at $79–$299. Founders' fundraising use is episodic (3–6 months every ~2 years), so expect churn at round close. Data on AI subscription retention shows AI products churn faster than non-AI ones even before episodic use is factored in.

### Cited Findings
**Price anchors (2026 list prices as reported by third parties; verify on vendor pages)**
- **DocSend:** Personal $15, Standard $65 and Advanced Data Rooms $300 per user per month on monthly billing. Standard is about $45 on annual billing. — [Pitchwise: DocSend pricing 2026](https://www.pitchwise.se/blog/docsend-pricing-2026)

**AI subscription retention benchmarks**

Summaries conflict on some weekly-plan numbers. — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/ai-apps-earn-41-more-per-user-but-churn-30-faster-revenuecat-finds/); [TechCrunch, Mar 10 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/10/ai-powered-apps-struggle-with-long-term-retention-new-report-shows)

### Inferences
- **Realistic price band for a solo-founder product:** about $19–$49/month per creator for the self-serve tier, anchored by Loom, HeyGen, Storydoc and DocSend Standard. A $99–$199/month team or agency tier is plausible if it includes multiple links, analytics, CRM hand-off and white-label, anchored by Chatbase Standard, Delphi Scaler and Qwilr seats. Pricing much above this would compete with Chatbase plus HeyGen bought together.
- **Fundraising-only positioning will behave like DocSend's raise-time usage:** subscribe for 1–6 months, then cancel. With roughly 2 years between rounds, the effective lifetime is a few months. That is incompatible with "solid MRR" unless pricing is a one-off or raise pass (for example $49–$149 per raise) or the product converts the customer into ongoing sales or marketing use.
- **Continuous segments are the only route to MRR:** B2B startups doing sales, agencies, consultants and real-estate teams use a link per prospect, client or listing every week. Even there, AI-tool churn runs higher than for non-AI tools (RevenueCat), so retention depends on making the link part of a recurring workflow: per-prospect personalized links, analytics on who watched or asked what, and CRM alerts.

### Gaps
- Calendly pricing was not retrieved because the search budget ran out.
- No public churn or retention data for DocSend, Gamma, HeyGen or Chatbase specifically. No B2B web-SaaS (ChartMogul-style) AI churn benchmark was found. RevenueCat's data is mobile B2C and may not transfer.
- No primary evidence of what founders would pay for an AI explainer link specifically (no pricing surveys or public MRR from close competitors were found in this scope).

## Q6. Which segment should be the beachhead for a solo founder, and why?

### Takeaway
On the evidence gathered, the strongest beachhead for MRR is early-stage B2B startups (seed to Series A, and their agencies) using the link for continuous prospect education and outbound. Fundraising should be used as an acquisition wedge and a one-time "raise pass", not as the core subscription. Founder fundraising alone has the most acute pain but structurally high churn and a skeptical, AI-averse audience.

### Cited Findings
- B2B buyers prefer rep-free buying (61% → 67%), avoid irrelevant outreach (73%), and still want seller input for fit questions. That creates a continuous, real job for a self-serve explainer link plus a human hand-off. — [Gartner, Jun 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience); [Digital Commerce 360, Mar 2026](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/03/17/gartner-b2b-buyers-rep-free-purchasing-ai-reshapes-sales/)
- Short video (<1 min, about 65% completion) and personalized video in outreach (vendor-reported reply lifts) are established sales behaviors with existing paid budgets (Loom $18–$24 per seat, HeyGen $29–$49). — [Vidico/Vidyard stats](https://vidico.com/news/b2b-video-marketing-statistics/); [Loom](https://www.loom.com/use-case/sales); [Trainn Loom pricing](https://trainn.co/blog/loom-pricing/)
- Fundraising: the pain is acute (contacting up to 100–200 investors, per 2020 DocSend data) but episodic (median 1.9 years between rounds). The audience says AI-generated materials hurt credibility. — [TechCrunch 2020](https://techcrunch.com/2020/03/03/docsends-new-pre-seed-data-shows-how-many-founders-you-should-have-and-how-many-investors-you-should-meet/); [Carta](https://carta.com/data/linkedin-time-between-startup-rounds-improving/); [Searchfunder](https://searchfunder.com/post/investor-perspective-ai-generated-pitch-decks-really-hurt-your-credibility); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91591431/ai-generated-pitch-decks)
- Agencies and consultants already pay per seat for interactive client-facing documents (Qwilr $35/user, positioned for agencies and consultants). — [Verdocs](https://verdocs.com/blog/qwilr-pricing)
- Buyers increasingly research via their own AI (51% start with AI chatbots) and use third-party GenAI 3x more than company bots. — [G2 via PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/new-g2-research-half-of-b2b-software-buyers-now-start-their-research-with-ai-chatbots-302742807.html); [The Next Web / Gartner](https://thenextweb.com/news/gartner-customers-third-party-genai-over-company-chatbots)

### Inferences
- **Recommended beachhead: seed to Series A B2B startups doing founder-led sales, especially technical or complex products.** They have a continuous need (every new prospect), they already pay for Loom, DocSend or Gamma, and the founder is time-starved, so "no recording" matters. Their audience (busy B2B buyers) prefers rep-free research, and the AI-made format carries less stigma with buyers than with VCs. Founder-led sales also means the same founder may want the link for fundraising, so the raise use comes as an add-on rather than as the reason to subscribe.
- **Second priority: small agencies (marketing, dev and consulting shops).** They make links for many clients, so each account becomes multi-link recurring revenue, and they can white-label the product.
- **Use fundraising and accelerators as a growth channel, not the revenue base.** Partner with accelerators to give free or discounted links to each cohort for demo day, which is cheap distribution to many founders, and offer a one-time raise pass. Do not rely on investors to engage with an AI agent. Keep the founder's real voice or slides, and label the agent as a deck assistant.
- **Deprioritize job seekers** (episodic, low willingness to pay, AI-screening audience) and **real estate** (good recurring per-listing job, but a different go-to-market and crowded with HeyGen and vertical tools) until the core segment works.
- **Design implications from viewer-side evidence:**
  - Keep the video under 1 minute.
  - Make the Q&A opt-in; never pop it up.
  - Always offer a human path (book a call).
  - Make the source content machine-readable so buyers' own AI assistants can use it.
  - Give owners analytics on who viewed and what they asked, since that is what keeps the link part of a recurring sales workflow.
  - Do not depend on LinkedIn link posts for virality, given the measured reach penalty ([MagicPost](https://magicpost.in/blog/linkedin-external-links-reach)).

### Gaps
- No direct customer-discovery evidence (interviews, waitlist conversion, pre-sales) for any segment. The beachhead recommendation is inferred from adjacent behavior and price data and should be validated with 15–20 interviews per top segment.
- Reddit and X/LinkedIn founder sentiment could not be collected (tool restrictions and search budget). Running those searches is the main open item for confirming demand language in the B2B-sales and agency segments.
- No data on how B2B buyers specifically react to an AI voice agent representing a vendor, as opposed to text chat.
