# Competitive Landscape: "Talk to my company/idea" AI agents (chat, voice, avatar) and direct "one link explains your startup" lookalikes, as of October 2026

Method note for the report writer: research was run on 2026-10-09. In this environment the page-fetch tool could not reach external sites (DNS and egress were blocked), so every finding below comes from extended web-search results that summarize the cited pages. I could not open the live pricing pages. Vendor pricing in particular should be read as "reported by the cited page around its stated date". Where sources disagreed, both are given. Data older than 2026 is flagged with its date.

## 1. Direct lookalikes: does anyone already sell "paste your content → shareable AI pitch page with video + Q&A agent"?

### Takeaway
Yes, partly. At least four small products already sell a link that turns a pitch deck into an AI Q&A experience for investors: DeckChat, Interactpitch, SendNow and dataroom.pro. Two of them add an avatar: Interactpitch for fundraising and Pitch Avatar for sales. None has disclosed funding or meaningful traction. No product found does the full combination of ingesting all of a founder's content, auto-generating an explainer video, and running a voice or avatar agent on one LinkedIn-ready link. Interactpitch and Pitch Avatar come closest. Founders are also doing this by hand with general tools, for example Perplexity's CEO and Polsia.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- The "paste deck → link → investor chat" wedge is already crowded with tiny, unfunded tools: DeckChat, SendNow, Interactpitch, dataroom.pro, Dataroomed, Peony and Tars. These sell for roughly €0–€99/mo or $9–52/mo. The low price points and lack of funding announcements suggest weak willingness to pay from founders for the Q&A feature alone.
- The "full combination" exists in pieces. Interactpitch combines deck, avatar, Q&A and analytics. Pitch Avatar combines deck, avatar, chat-avatar and lead capture. DeckChat combines deck, chat and a share link. No product found combines (a) ingesting all of a founder's content beyond the deck, (b) auto-generating an explainer video, and (c) a voice or video agent on one public, LinkedIn-friendly link.
- The French Miria/Fred24 LinkedIn launch shows demand-side curiosity for "talk to my AI clone" posts on LinkedIn specifically. It is positioned as mentorship or monetization, not as "explain my startup".
- Polsia and the Perplexity CEO show high-profile founders already using AI as their investor interface. The PitchBook note that some VCs find this rude is a real adoption risk for the fundraising use case.

### Gaps
- I could not open Product Hunt, DeckChat, Interactpitch or dataroom.pro directly, so upvotes, review counts, maker names and exact launch dates are unverified.
- No Show HN, YC-directory or Indie Hackers launch exactly matching "paste content → video + voice agent pitch link" was found. Searches for "AI pitch room", "talk to my startup", "AI investor agent" and "pitch deck chatbot" surfaced only the products above. This is either a real gap or a limit of the search index.
- No public traction metrics (MRR, users) were found for DeckChat, Interactpitch, SendNow, dataroom.pro or Tars.
- The PitchBook Polsia article date and the full context of investor reactions could not be verified.

## 2. Digital-twin and clone platforms (Delphi, Tavus, HeyGen LiveAvatar, D-ID, Synthesia, Coachvox and others): pricing and positioning

### Takeaway
Clone and avatar infrastructure is well funded and cheap enough to rent per minute: roughly $0.10–0.37 per avatar-minute and about $0.08 per voice-minute plus LLM costs. Delphi is the closest "talk to my mind" consumer product and already offers text, voice and video calls (through Tavus) on a shareable profile. Its positioning is experts and creators monetizing subscriptions, not founders explaining companies. Tavus and HeyGen LiveAvatar both give a developer a shareable or embeddable live-avatar conversation trained on uploaded documents.

### Inferences
- The avatar and voice layer is a commodity rented from well-funded vendors: HeyGen ($200M ARR), Synthesia ($4B), Tavus ($40M B), D-ID and ElevenLabs. A solo founder would be a reseller of this layer, not a differentiator on it.
- Rough unit costs for a 5-minute visitor conversation: about $1.60–1.85 on Tavus overage (5 × $0.32–0.37), about $0.50–1.00 on HeyGen LiveAvatar (5 × $0.10–0.20), and about $0.40 plus LLM on ElevenLabs voice (5 × $0.08). Costs scale with viral LinkedIn traffic, so a free or freemium "post it on LinkedIn" model has real cost exposure unless avatar minutes are gated. These figures are computed from the per-minute rates cited above.
- Delphi already delivers "talk to my clone by text, voice or video on a shareable link" with a free tier. It lacks company-explainer framing, auto-generated explainer video and investor or sales analytics. A founder could use Delphi today for much of the "talk to me" half.
- Sales-focused avatar agents (1mind, Supersonik) raised real money because they tie to pipeline and revenue. That is the demand signal for "an agent that represents a company to the public".

### Gaps
- Delphi's official pricing page could not be opened, and third-party pricing conflicts.
- No 2026 user, revenue or retention data was found for Tavus, Coachvox or Sensay.
- Whether HeyGen LiveAvatar offers a no-code public share link, as opposed to an embed or API, was not confirmed.
- Character-style consumer clone platforms (Character.ai and similar) were not researched in depth. They are not oriented to company explanation.

## 3. No-code chat and voice agent builders trained on company docs and shared via a link: how much of the idea can a founder get for free?

### Takeaway
A founder can get much of the idea for $0–40 without a specialist product. Google NotebookLM, now "Gemini Notebook", offers a free public link with a chat-only mode plus AI audio and video overviews generated from pasted content, so it covers both halves (explainer video and Q&A) at a basic level. Chatbase gives a hosted, shareable agent page. ElevenLabs Agents' free tier includes a knowledge base and a widget for a voice agent. Tavus, D-ID and Delphi have free avatar or clone tiers. OpenAI custom GPTs and Claude artifacts are weaker options: GPT creation is closed to personal accounts and GPTs are being retired, and Claude's AI-powered artifacts require viewers to sign in. What the free options lack is polish, branding, a founder avatar or voice, lead capture, question analytics and a single "LinkedIn-native" experience.

## 4. AI sales rooms, buyer enablement and data rooms: do they include buyer-facing AI Q&A agents?

## 6. What would be genuinely differentiated versus all of the above?

### Takeaway
"Upload content → link → chat or avatar Q&A" is not differentiated on its own: it exists in DeckChat, Interactpitch, SendNow, Pitch Avatar, Delphi and NotebookLM for free. Differentiation would have to come from what the competitors visibly lack. Those gaps are: (1) answer quality and founder control, (2) a question-intelligence and lead loop back to the founder, (3) a LinkedIn-native, anonymous, zero-friction viewing experience, (4) being machine-readable for investors' or buyers' own AI agents, and (5) a use case with ROI beyond fundraising.

### Cited Findings (evidence for each gap)
- **Answer quality and founder control**: Interactpitch's Product Hunt feedback reported weak answers on rephrased questions, false "outside the deck" refusals, and a request for founder-added FAQs. — [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/interactpitch-2)
- **Question analytics as the value**: Interactpitch and SendNow already surface "what questions came up" to the sender, and Tars captures leads. This feature exists but sits in small tools. — [Toolify](https://www.toolify.ai/tool/interactpitch); [SendNow](https://sendnow.live/blog/docsend-vs-sendnow); [Tars](https://hellotars.com/ai-agents/pitch-deck-ai-agent)
- **Viewer-friction gap in free tools**: Claude AI artifacts require viewer sign-in and bill the viewer. GPT creation is closed to personal accounts and is being retired. NotebookLM chat-only view is "not a security boundary". — [Claude Help](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11649427-use-artifacts-to-visualize-and-create-ai-apps-without-ever-writing-a-line-of-code); [OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8798878-sharing-and-publishing-gpts); [Google Help](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16322204?hl=en)
- **LinkedIn as a launch surface for clones**: Fred24 was revealed live on LinkedIn, and other French Tech founders followed within days. — [Cap Entreprendre](https://www.capentreprendre.fr/ces-pionniers-de-la-french-tech-devoilent-leur-clone-ia-revolutionnaire-pour-accompagner-les-entrepreneurs/)
- **Agent-readable companies**: YC's Summer 2026 Requests for Startups include "software for agents", meaning machine-readable interfaces (APIs, MCPs, CLIs) agents can use without a human. DocSend is beta-testing an MCP connector for data rooms. Polsia shows agents already participating in fundraising. — [The VC Corner](https://www.thevccorner.com/p/yc-summer-2026-requests-for-startups-ideas); [Peony blog](https://www.peony.ink/blog/top-10-alternatives-to-docsend); [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/want-to-invest-in-my-startup-talk-to-my-agent)
- **ROI-linked use cases attract capital**: 1mind (website and deal-room avatar sellers, $40M), Supersonik (demo agent, $5M a16z) and Aligned (buyer copilot, $60M B). — [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/gemmaallen/2026/01/25/inside-1minds-40m-ai-bet-and-the-superhuman-vision-behind-it/); [EU-Startups](https://www.eu-startups.com/2025/09/spanish-startup-supersonik-launches-from-stealth-and-raises-e4-2-million-for-its-multilingual-ai-agent/); [Everything PR](https://everything-pr.com/aligned-raises-60-million-series-b-for-ai-b2b-sales-workspace)

### Inferences
These are the researcher's synthesis, not sourced facts.
- **Likely not differentiated**: the avatar or voice itself (rented from Tavus, HeyGen, D-ID or ElevenLabs); chat over a deck (DeckChat, SendNow, Peony); a generated explainer video (NotebookLM free, Gamma, Synthesia, HeyGen); a share link with view analytics (DocSend, Gamma, Storydoc).
- **Plausibly differentiated, in rough order of defensibility:**
  1. **Founder-in-the-loop answer governance.** Approved answers, "I don't know, I'll ask the founder" escalation that notifies the founder and later updates the knowledge base, refusal of off-limits topics (valuation, confidential metrics), and per-audience disclosure tiers (public, investor, customer). This directly addresses the Interactpitch complaints and the risk of the agent misrepresenting the company.
  2. **Question intelligence.** An aggregated digest of what visitors asked, where the pitch is unclear and which objections recur, plus visitor identification and lead or calendar handoff. This turns a vanity link into a pipeline or fundraising CRM input. It exists only in thin form today.
  3. **Truly frictionless public viewing.** No sign-in, fast mobile load, a strong LinkedIn link preview, and a video-first landing with "ask anything" as the second act. Horizontal assistants are moving away from anonymous public links.
  4. **Agent-readable company profile.** The same content exposed as an MCP or structured endpoint so investors' and buyers' diligence agents can query it. This fits YC's "software for agents" request and the Polsia-style trend. No competitor found positions this way for startups.
  5. **Use-case focus with ROI.** Founder-led sales (prospects), recruiting (candidates) or accelerator and demo-day cohorts (bulk, B2B2C) may monetize better than fundraising alone, which is episodic and where some investors dislike agents.
- **Cost design matters**: given $0.08–0.37 per conversation-minute in infrastructure costs, a viral free tier should default to text plus pre-rendered video, with live avatar or voice minutes metered or paid.
- **Overall crowding verdict for the agent half**: crowded at the feature level (more than 10 tools and free substitutes) but empty at the brand or category level for founders. No funded company owns "the one link that explains your startup", which could be either white space or a sign of weak demand. The evidence (no funded lookalikes, the Tome precedent, VC friction) leans toward weak standalone demand for the fundraising use case.

### Gaps
- No direct customer-demand evidence (surveys, waitlists, conversion data) for founders paying for a "pitch link with agent" was found.
- Securities and regulatory constraints on AI agents answering investor questions publicly (for example, US general-solicitation rules or misstatement liability) were not researched and could matter for the fundraising use case.
- How LinkedIn treats such links (preview rendering, reach suppression for external links) was not researched.
